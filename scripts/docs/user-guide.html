01 Introduction

Welcome to EventFlow.

A multi-tenant platform for universities, companies, and communities that want to publish events, collect registrations, take payments, and run check-in — without five different SaaS tools duct-taped together.

Attendees

Discover & go

Browse events, register in under a minute, carry tickets in your pocket, show a QR at the door.

Organizers

Publish & run

Build an event with a 6-step wizard, share it with a link, check people in with a phone camera, issue certificates after.

Admins

Oversee everything

Manage members, billing, payments, refunds, the public website, feature modules — all from one dashboard.

EventFlow01 · Welcome

What you'll do on this platform

EventFlow centralizes the full lifecycle of an event: from the first announcement, through the moment someone buys a ticket, to when they walk through the door and later download a certificate. The same app adapts to who's logged in — a student sees the browsing and ticket side; an organizer sees the admin dashboard; a super admin sees every tenant on the platform.

If you've ever used Eventbrite for listings, Stripe for payments, a custom QR scanner for check-in, and a separate mailer for certificates — this is all four, stitched together properly.

What makes this different

  • Multi-tenant — one EventFlow deployment can host a whole university, with clubs, departments, and student organizations all running their events under separate subdomains.
  • Built-in website builder — each organization gets a customizable public site with drag-and-drop sections, not just a profile page inside a directory.
  • Feature modules — 12 plug-in features (live quiz, live poll, leaderboard, photo gallery, blog, countdown timer, weather widget, team profiles, sponsor showcase, volunteer sign-up, merchandise store, event calendar) that clip into any event.
  • Role-based access — nine granular roles so a Finance Manager can refund but not publish, a Coordinator can check in but not cancel, a Viewer can audit without changing anything.

Three words that mean different things here

Enterprise — the top container, like a whole university or company. Organization — a sub-group within an enterprise: a club, a department, a team. Event — an individual happening with a date, venue, and registration.

EventFlow02 · Getting started

Create your account

  1. Open /signup. Fill your full name, email, a password that satisfies the live strength indicator, and your phone number.
  2. Tick the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy checkbox. Both open in a new tab — you can read them without losing your form state.
  3. Optionally fill in college or company. These are editable later from your profile.
  4. You'll receive a 6-digit code by email. Enter it on the verification screen. Code is valid for 10 minutes; five wrong attempts invalidates it and you'll need a fresh one.
  5. Verified account lands you at /dashboard.

Signing in

The login screen has two tabs — pick whichever you prefer.

Email + Password

The classic path. If you forgot your password, the Forgot your password? link sits directly under the password field — not hidden in a footer.

Login with OTP

Enter email, receive a code, enter the code. Useful when you don't remember your password or you're on a borrowed device and don't want to type it.

Tip

The Sign in with Google and Sign in with Microsoft buttons below the tabs work if your administrator has enabled SSO. No separate password to remember.

Didn't receive the OTP?

Check your spam folder first. Then hit Resend OTP (available again after 30 seconds). If you're in a local development environment where email isn't configured, the code can be read directly from the OTP row in your database.

03 Register for events

Three paths, one polished flow.

Free events end in a QR. Paid events route through Razorpay. Team events build and join on invite codes. The wizard in the middle is the same.

EventFlow03 · Registering

The registration wizard, at a glance

Every event uses the same three-step form, regardless of price or format. The steps slide in with a light horizontal animation so forward progress feels tangible.

Free event — the short path

  1. On the event page, the sidebar says Register for Free. Click it.
  2. Step 1 — pick a ticket type (most free events have only one).
  3. Step 2 — your name and email are pre-filled if you're logged in. Fill any custom form fields the organizer added (year of study, dietary preference, etc.).
  4. Step 3 — review, agree to the event's T&C, click Confirm Registration.
  5. Success screen shows the event title, full date, venue, a QR code, Add to Calendar, Download Ticket, and a "what happens next" checklist.
EventFlow03 · Registering

Paid event — with a coupon

  1. On Step 1, after picking your ticket type, a Coupon Code field appears below.
  2. Enter the code and click Apply. The line-item total updates with the discount shown.
  3. Steps 2 and 3 are identical to the free flow. On the Review step, the button reads Pay ₹ NNN with your post-discount total.
  4. Razorpay's payment sheet opens in an overlay. Use your card, UPI, net banking, or wallet.
  5. On a successful payment, the success screen renders and your ticket is live in /dashboard/tickets. The same ticket carries the QR you'll show at the door.

Payment didn't complete?

If the Razorpay sheet closes without success, your registration sits in Pending. Go to /dashboard/payments and click Retry Payment. The same gateway order is re-used, so you won't be double-charged.

Team event

Some events allow (or require) team registration. You'll see Team registration · 2–4 members on the event page. The flow branches:

Team leader

Create team — you name it, you're given an invite code like AB7C-3XZP. Share the code with your teammates. The code expires at the event's end date.

Teammate

Join team — enter the code. If the team isn't full and the code hasn't expired, you're added. You'll appear in the leader's roster in the admin dashboard.

Once you're logged in, /dashboard is your home base. Tickets, saved events, certificates, payments — and animated counters that tell you, at a glance, how you've used the platform.

EventFlow04 · Dashboard

What's where

/dashboardHome. Live counters, upcoming-events list with "in 3 days" countdowns, quick actions.
/dashboard/eventsEvery event you've registered for — upcoming, past, cancelled. You can cancel here if the event hasn't started.
/dashboard/ticketsActive tickets. Click one to open the full-screen QR you'll show at the venue.
/dashboard/savedEvents you've hearted but haven't registered for yet.
/dashboard/certificatesCertificates issued after you attended an event — downloadable.
/dashboard/paymentsEvery payment made through EventFlow with status and receipt.
/dashboard/profileYour personal details, avatar, linked social accounts.
/dashboard/settingsNotification preferences, password change, two-factor auth.
/dashboard/notificationsInbox for platform-generated notifications (approval granted, payment received, etc.).

Tickets work offline

Your tickets are cached by a service worker. Even with no signal at the venue, /dashboard/tickets opens, the QR renders, and the gate scanner validates the signature when the network returns. An amber banner explains you're offline — the QR still works.

05 Running your own

From draft to live,
in six steps.

Own an event? The creation wizard is six guided steps. You can save a draft at any point, preview it as an attendee would see it, and only publish when you're ready.

EventFlow05 · Organize

First, make sure you have an org

Everything you publish lives under an organization. If you don't have one yet, open /admin/create-org, fill in the name, URL slug, category, and contact email. You become that org's admin automatically.

Email verification required

Creating an organization requires a verified email. It's a deliberate gate against spam orgs that rent storage and never publish an event.

The six steps of event creation

Step 1   Basic Title, event URL (slug), visibility (public / unlisted / invite-only), category, tags, short and long descriptions.
Step 2   Date & Venue Start and end datetimes, timezone, either an in-person venue (address) or an online platform (Zoom, Teams, or custom URL).
Step 3   Tickets One or more ticket types with price, total tickets available, max tickets per person, optional sale window. Early-bird and regular are common.
Step 4   Form Custom form fields asked during registration — text, select, checkbox, file upload. Mark fields required as needed.
Step 5   Media Cover image, gallery, FAQs, sponsor logos, rules and terms, refund policy.
Step 6   Review Everything on one page. Preview opens the public event page with a yellow Preview mode banner so you can check the attendee view without publishing.

Saving drafts

Every step has a Save as Draft button. Drafts don't appear in the public catalogue — only people with ?preview=1 and edit rights can see them.

06 At the venue

Show up. Scan in. Get on with it.

Check-in takes about two seconds per attendee. Works offline if the venue Wi-Fi doesn't.

EventFlow06 · The venue

As an attendee

  1. Pull up /dashboard/tickets. Tap the ticket for the event you're at.
  2. Tap Full-screen QR. The screen goes dark, the code fills the display.
  3. The gate team scans it. You're in. The ticket now says Checked In with a timestamp.

As an organizer / coordinator

  1. Open /admin/org/events/[id]/check-in on a phone or tablet. Allow camera access the first time.
  2. Tap QR Scan. Point the back camera at each attendee's ticket. A green confirmation plays; the check-in count ticks up.
  3. If someone's QR is broken, tap Manual. Search by name, email, or registration number. Click Check In on the row.
  4. Live stats above the scanner — total expected, currently checked-in, percentage — update as people stream in.

After the event

  • The organizer can bulk-issue certificates from /admin/org/certificates. They show up in each attendee's /dashboard/certificates.
  • The attendee gets an automatic email prompt to fill feedback at /events/[slug]/feedback. Organizers see aggregated results.
  • Every attendee and every organizer can verify the authenticity of a certificate or ticket at /verify/[code] — a public lookup by the short verification code printed on it.

07 Troubleshooting

If something feels off.

The short list of "why isn't this working" questions, and what to do about each.

EventFlow07 · Troubleshooting
"Event is full"All seats taken before you got there. If the event has a waitlist, your registration is queued and you'll be promoted automatically if someone cancels.
"You are already registered for this event"Your account has an existing registration. Open /dashboard/events — it's probably sitting in Pending or Confirmed.
QR won't scan at the venueAsk the check-in person to switch to Manual and look up your registration number. Same result, slower.
Forgot password/forgot-password → enter email → receive a 6-digit code → set a new password.
Can't see an event you registered forIt may have been cancelled. Cancelled events are hidden from the public catalogue but still appear in /dashboard/events under the Cancelled tab.
Payment charged but no ticketCheck /dashboard/payments. If the payment status says COMPLETED but the ticket isn't there, click the row and use Regenerate Ticket. If still stuck, contact the organizer (their email is on the event page).
Coupon rejectedReasons in order of likelihood: wrong code, expired, usage limit reached, doesn't apply to the ticket type you picked. The error message will say which.
"Too many attempts" on OTPFive wrong tries invalidates the code. Request a new one from the same screen.

Where to get help

Event-specific questions — payment didn't reach the organizer, a certificate is missing, the venue moved — use the contact email on the event page. The organizer is usually faster than platform support.

Platform-level issues — your account is locked, a payment got lost in the gateway, a certificate verification returns 404 — go to /contact and submit a ticket with the details.
