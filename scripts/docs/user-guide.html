01 Chapter one · Introduction

One place for the whole event.

Listing, tickets, payments, check-in and certificates, in one app instead of five tools taped together.

Attendees

Find & go

Browse events, register in a minute, keep the ticket on your phone, show the QR at the door.

Organizers

Publish & run

Build an event in six steps, share the link, scan people in with a phone camera, send certificates after.

Admins

Keep it in order

Members, payments, refunds, the public website and add-on modules, from one admin area.

What you can do here

The app changes with who's signed in. An attendee sees events and their tickets. An organizer also gets the admin area for their organization. A platform admin sees every organization on the platform.

Eventbrite for the listing, a payment link, a QR app at the gate and a mail merge for certificates. EventFlow does all four.

Worth knowing

  • Many organizations, one platform. A university can run every club and department on one install, each with its own public site.
  • A website builder. Each organization gets a public site built from drag-and-drop sections.
  • Add-on modules. 12 of them: live quiz, live poll, leaderboard, photo gallery, blog, countdown, weather, team profiles, sponsors, volunteer sign-up, merch store and an event calendar.
  • Roles. Nine of them, so the person handling refunds doesn't also need the power to delete events.

Three words with a specific meaning here

Enterprise: the top level, like a whole university or company. Organization: a group inside it, such as a club or a department. Event: one happening, with a date, a venue and registrations.

02 Chapter two · Getting started

Your first five minutes.

Create an account, confirm your email, sign in.

Create your account

  1. Open /signup. Enter your name, email, phone and a password. A strength meter tells you when the password is good enough.
  2. Tick the box for the Terms and the Privacy Policy. Both links open in a new tab, so your form stays filled in.
  3. College or company is optional. You can add it later in your profile.
  4. We email you a 6-digit code. Type it in. It works for 10 minutes, and five wrong tries cancel it, after which you ask for a new one.
  5. Done. You land on /dashboard.

Signing in

The sign-in page has two tabs.

Tab 1

Email + password

The usual way. Forgot your password? sits right under the password field.

Tab 2

Login with OTP

Type your email, get a code, type the code. Handy on a borrowed laptop, or when the password has slipped your mind.

Google and Microsoft

The Sign in with Google and Sign in with Microsoft buttons appear only if the people running your EventFlow have switched them on.

No code in your inbox?

Look in spam first. Resend OTP unlocks after 30 seconds.

03 Chapter three · Registering

Pick a ticket. Fill it in. You're in.

Free, paid or team event, the form is the same three steps.

step 1TicketPick a type, add a coupon
step 2Your detailsName, email, the organizer's questions
step 3ReviewCheck it, confirm or pay
doneYour ticketQR code, calendar file, PDF

A free event

  1. On the event page, hit the register button in the side panel.
  2. Ticket: choose a ticket type. Many free events have just one.
  3. Your details: name and email are filled in if you're signed in. Answer any questions the organizer added, such as year of study or food preference.
  4. Review: accept the event's terms and press Confirm registration.
  5. Your ticket appears with its QR code, an add-to-calendar file and a PDF download.

A paid event, with a coupon

  1. On the Ticket step, type the code into the coupon field and press Apply. The total updates and shows the discount.
  2. On Review, the button shows the amount, for example Pay ₹449. Razorpay opens: card, UPI, net banking or wallet.
  3. Once the payment goes through, the ticket shows up in /dashboard/tickets.

Your seat is held for 15 minutes

When you start paying, the seat is kept for you for 15 minutes. If the payment window closes, go to /dashboard/events: Complete payment takes you back (the button then says Retry payment), and Release seat lets it go. After 15 minutes unpaid, the seat goes back on sale automatically.

A team event

Some events take teams. The event page says so, along with the team size.

Team lead

Create a team

Name it and you get an invite code. Send the code to your teammates.

Teammate

Join a team

Enter the code. If the team has room and the code is still valid, you're added.

04 Chapter four · Your dashboard

Everything you've signed up for.

/dashboard is home once you're signed in.

What's where

/dashboardCounters and your upcoming events, with countdowns.
/dashboard/eventsEverything you've registered for, under Upcoming, Past and All. Cancel here before an event starts, or finish a payment you left halfway.
/dashboard/ticketsYour tickets. Full screen blows the QR up for the gate. You can also add the event to your calendar, download a PDF, or use Transfer ticket.
/dashboard/savedEvents you've hearted.
/dashboard/certificatesCertificates from events you attended, ready to download.
/dashboard/paymentsEvery payment, its status and its receipt.
/dashboard/profileName, phone, city, bio, college, company and designation.
/dashboard/settingsNotifications, password, Download my data and Delete my account.
/dashboard/notificationsMessages from the platform and from organizers.

Giving a ticket away

Transfer ticket

Enter your friend's email. They get a fresh ticket number and QR code, and your old QR stops working.

No signal at the venue?

Tickets on your phone

Tickets you've opened are kept on your device, so /dashboard/tickets still shows them offline. They're cleared when you sign out.

Your data is yours

Download my data in Settings gives you everything we hold about you as a file. Delete my account removes the account for good.

05 Chapter five · Organizing

From draft to live in six steps.

Save a draft whenever you like, preview what attendees will see, publish when it's ready.

First, you need an organization

Every event belongs to an organization. If you don't have one, open /admin/create-org and fill in the name, web address, category and contact email. You become its admin. Your email has to be verified first.

The six steps

1  Basic InfoTitle, web address, category, tags, descriptions and visibility: Public, Private, Invite Only or Unlisted.
2  Date & VenueStart and end time, time zone, and a venue address or an online link.
3  TicketsTicket types with price, quantity, a per-person limit and an optional sale window.
4  Registration FormExtra questions for attendees (text, choices, checkboxes, file upload), each optional or required.
5  Media & DetailsCover image, FAQs, sponsors, rules and refund policy.
6  Review & PublishEverything on one page. Preview opens the public page with a "Preview mode" bar and a link back to the admin area.

Drafts

Save as draft

On every step. Drafts never appear in the public list. Only people who can edit the event can preview them.

Approval

Pending approval

If your enterprise checks events before they go live, publishing sends the event for approval first. You'll see when it's approved.

06 Chapter six · On the day

Scan. Beep. Next.

A couple of seconds per person at the door.

If you're attending

  1. Open /dashboard/tickets and tap the ticket.
  2. Press Full screen so the QR fills the screen.
  3. Someone scans it. You're in.

If you're on the door

  1. Open the event's check-in page, /admin/org/events/[id]/check-in, on a phone. Allow the camera.
  2. Point it at each QR. A green confirmation appears and the count goes up.
  3. A QR won't scan? Search by name, email or registration number and check them in by hand.

After the event

  • The organizer issues certificates from /admin/org/certificates. They show up in each attendee's /dashboard/certificates.
  • Attendees can leave feedback at /events/[slug]/feedback, and organizers see the results.
  • Anyone can check that a certificate is genuine at /verify, using the code printed on it.
07 Chapter seven · Troubleshooting

When something's off.

The usual suspects, and what to do about each.

"Event is full"Every seat is taken. If the event keeps a waitlist, you're on it, and the organizer moves people across by hand when seats free up.
"You are already registered"You have a registration already. Look in /dashboard/events: it may still be waiting for payment.
Paid, but no ticketCheck /dashboard/payments. If the payment shows as complete but there's no ticket, contact the organizer (their email is on the event page).
Payment window closedComplete payment in /dashboard/events within 15 minutes. After that, register again.
QR won't scanAsk the door team to look you up by name or registration number.
Forgot password/forgot-password: enter your email, type the code, choose a new password.
Coupon rejectedWrong code, expired, used up, or not valid for that ticket type. The message tells you which.
"Too many attempts"Five wrong codes cancel the code. Ask for a new one on the same screen.
Want a refundIf the event is cancelled, you get a full refund automatically. Otherwise ask the organizer. The rules are at /refunds.

Who to ask

For anything about a particular event (payments, certificates, a venue change), email the organizer. Their address is on the event page, and they usually reply faster.

For problems with your account or the platform itself, use /contact.
